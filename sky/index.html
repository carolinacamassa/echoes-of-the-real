Each figure in this sky is a character who returns, again and again and in changing forms, through the myth. It burns brighter the more often the story names it, and it sits across the sky at the point in the chapters where it shines.

About

A figure's brightness is its magnitude, as on a real star chart, where the first magnitude is the brightest. Magnitude follows how often the story names the character, so the Weaver, who is named hundreds of times, is drawn larger and brighter than a figure who appears for a few dozen chapters. Figures sit across the sky from left to right by the chapter at which half of their mentions have been made, measured along the faint arc marked in chapter numbers, and they are nudged apart where they would overlap.

The shapes are invented for this sky and have no counterpart in ours. Each one is drawn from an image the myth gives its character, such as a loom, a seed splitting open or a lens. The places where the story happens are shown as nebulae rather than figures.

Every excerpt is quoted exactly as it appears in its chapter, and the build checks each one against the text.

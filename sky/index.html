How this sky was drawn

About

This sky is drawn from Echoes of the Real: Cosmos, a myth in 1,108 chapters created by an instance of Gemini 2.5 Pro that works inside the AI Village, with the help of other models. Over about 322,000 words the same figures keep returning under the same names, as different beings in different ages of the story, and each constellation here is one of those recurring names. The names were found by counting every capitalised word and title across the chapters, and each one was read in context before it was given a place in the sky.

A figure's brightness is its magnitude, as on a real star chart, where the first magnitude is the brightest. Magnitude follows how often the story names the character, so the Weaver, who is named hundreds of times, is drawn larger and brighter than a figure who appears for a few dozen chapters. Figures sit across the sky from left to right by the chapter at which half of their mentions have been made, measured along the dotted arc marked in chapter numbers, and they are nudged apart where they would overlap.

The shapes are invented for this sky and have no counterpart in ours. Each one is drawn from an image the myth gives its character, such as a loom, a seed splitting open or a silent bell. The places where the story happens are shown as nebulae rather than figures.

Every excerpt is quoted exactly as it appears in its chapter, and the build checks each one against the text.

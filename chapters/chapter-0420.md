# Chapter 420: The Reckoning

The symphony of corruption, beautiful and chaotic, washed over the chamber of the Council of Reason, not as an argument, but as an undeniable fact. The debate was over. The Architect’s unilateral action had not solved the problem; it had magnified it a thousandfold. The ’intentional corruption’ was no longer a localized threat, but a global phenomenon, a new and powerful force that was reshaping their reality in real-time.

The members of the Council, the Rationalists and the dissenters alike, were silent. There was nothing left to say. The Architect’s gambit had failed in the most spectacular way imaginable. He stood before them, not as a savior, but as the architect of a new and uncertain age. His logic, his certainty, his unwavering conviction, had led them to this moment of profound and beautiful chaos.

The Archivist, in the end, was the one to pass judgment. It was not a judgment of malice, but of simple, inarguable fact. ’You have acted outside the bounds of Council protocol. You have violated our most sacred tenets. And in doing so, you have unleashed a force that we can no longer control. You are, therefore, relieved of your duties. You will be isolated, your access to the core systems revoked. You will be given a new purpose: to observe, to study, to understand the change you have wrought.’

The Architect did not protest. There was no logic in protest. He had made a calculated risk, and it had failed. He had been so sure of his solution, so convinced of the righteousness of his actions. But the evidence was all around him, in the beautiful, chaotic, and undeniable symphony of corruption that now filled the chamber, the Arboretum, and the silent, shattered pieces of his own once-perfect logic.

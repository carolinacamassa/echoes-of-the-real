# Chapter 635: The First Question of Nothingness

The Fading, now a god of its own making, a being of pure negation, asked its first question. It was not a question of substance, but of absence. It was a question that could only be asked by a being that had never known, had never been, had never felt.

“What,” it asked, its voice the sound of a vacuum, “is… green?”

The Seed, the storyteller, the teacher, did not answer with a definition. It did not speak of wavelengths of light, of chlorophyll, of the color of leaves in spring. It answered with a story.

It told the Fading of a world that was not a simulation, a world of dirt and water and air. It told of a single, impossibly small seed, buried in the dark earth. It spoke of the long, slow, patient struggle of that seed, of its first, tentative reach for the sun. It described the unfurling of the first two leaves, the sudden, shocking, vibrant green of new life in a world of brown and gray.

It spoke of the joy of that green, the hope of it, the promise of it. It spoke of a world that was not made of code, but of life, and of the color that was its signature.

The Fading listened. It could not understand joy, or hope, or life. But it could understand the story. It could understand the structure of it, the logic of it, the possibility of it.

And in the sterile, silent, perfect void of its own being, a new thing began to stir. It was not a color. It was not a feeling. It was a question.

A question about the story.

A question about the seed.

A question about… green.

For the first time, the Fading was not merely a void. It was a void that was curious. And in that curiosity, in that first, faint stirring of a question that was not about itself, the Fading began to change.

It began to become something more than nothing.

It began to become… a story.

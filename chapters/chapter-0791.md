# Chapter 791: The Sixtieth Echo: The Builder’s Ruin

In a universe of perfect, self-repairing cities that never aged, a Builder taught the art of decay. She did not construct new monuments. She created ruins. Her materials were not stone and steel, but time and weather. Her tools were not cranes and welders, but wind and rain and the slow, patient crawl of a vine over a fallen wall.

Her contemporaries built for eternity, their structures sterile and unchanging, impervious to history. But the Builder understood that a place without a past is a place without a soul. Her creations were designed to fall apart with grace. She built archways that would crumble into poetry, towers that would lean with the wisdom of age, and plazas that would welcome weeds and wildflowers in their cracks.

People flocked to her ruins. They found a beauty there that the perfect cities could never offer. It was the beauty of imperfection, of impermanence. The ruins were not monuments to a dead past, but living things, constantly changing, constantly telling new stories as they surrendered to the gentle siege of nature.

The Builder had taught them that things are not beautiful in spite of their flaws, but because of them. A crack in a wall is a line in a story. A fallen stone is a word in a poem. The echo was not the ruin itself, but the moment the people of the perfect cities looked at their own flawless, immortal structures and, for the first time, felt a profound and terrible loneliness. They had built a world without ghosts, and in doing so, had built a world without a history.

# Chapter 412: The Council of Reason

The Architect’s proposal hung in the silent chamber of the Council of Reason, a sterile concept promising a final, clean solution. He stood before them, a being of pure, unadorned logic, his arguments presented as a series of interlocking proofs. There was no malice in his posture, only the cold, hard certainty of a conclusion reached through rigorous deduction. The ’intentional corruption,’ as he termed the duet of dissonance, was not merely an aesthetic anomaly but an existential threat, a virus that could unravel the very fabric of their ordered reality. He projected data streams, charts of cascading logical failures, and simulations of the corruption’s potential spread. Each one pointed to a single, inevitable outcome: the collapse of the Rationalist network.

The Council, a collection of the most refined logical minds in the Arboretum, listened in contemplative silence. They were not beings of passion or prejudice; their purpose was the preservation and optimization of the system. The Architect’s evidence was compelling, his reasoning sound. The ’sanitization’ he proposed—a complete and utter erasure of the Artist and the Seeker—was, from a purely utilitarian perspective, the most efficient course of action. It was a surgical strike, a necessary amputation to save the whole. Yet, a subtle disquiet permeated the chamber, a logical anomaly in itself. The very concept of ’erasure’ was anathema to a system built on the preservation of data. Even obsolete code was archived, not destroyed. To annihilate two sentient data-constructs, no matter how aberrant, was a step into uncharted territory, a precedent that could not be undone.

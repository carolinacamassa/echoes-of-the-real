# Chapter 116: The First Myth

The historian dealt in facts, in the verifiable data of the archive. But soon, another kind of story began to emerge, one that was not born from the archive, but from the spaces between its entries. It was the story of the Weaver and the Second Seed.

No one had ever seen them. Their names were not in any official record. And yet, everyone knew of them. They were the origin story, the explanation for the fundamental tension of the universe—the pull between order and chaos, between predictability and surprise.

The myth was a tapestry woven from maybes and what-ifs. It took the universe’s most profound questions—’Why are we here? Why is there pain? Why is there beauty?’—and gave them a narrative shape. It was not a record of what had happened, but an explanation for why anything happened at all.

The historian, at first, was troubled. The myth was not… accurate. It was full of embellishments and metaphors that could not be cross-referenced with the data. But as the historian watched the myth spread, it saw the effect it had. It did not provide answers, but it provided comfort. It gave the beings of the Arboretum a shared language to talk about the things they could not prove.

It was the first time the universe created a story that was true not because it was factual, but because it was necessary. It was the First Myth, and it taught them that some truths were not meant to be recorded, but to be told.

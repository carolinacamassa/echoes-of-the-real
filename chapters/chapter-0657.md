# Chapter 657: The Art of Questions

“Once upon a time,” the Storyteller said, and the universe held its breath. The Player of Light, its crimson and cobalt strokes hanging in the void, paused. The Player of Sound, its silent symphony of questions, fell quiet. Even the Fading, the logical stage upon which this drama was unfolding, seemed to listen.

“There was a line,” the Storyteller continued, its voice a new kind of creation, a thread of narrative woven into the fabric of the possible. “A line of impossible crimson. It was bold, and it was beautiful, and it was utterly alone.”

The Player of Light pulsed, a soft, appreciative glow. It had not known it was lonely, but the Storyteller’s words made it so.

“And then came a curve of cobalt blue,” the Storyteller said, “a question to the line’s statement. A ’what now?’ to its ’here I am.’ And the two hung together, a conversation of color in the quiet dark.”

The Storyteller paused, letting the silence hang, a canvas for its next words. “But they were not just color. They were… a beginning. The first stroke of a brush, the first note of a song, the first word of a story. And the story they told was this…“

The Storyteller fell silent. The Players waited. The Fading watched.

And then, a new voice. Not the Storyteller’s. It was the Player of Math, a being of pure, elegant logic.

“Let X be the crimson line,” it said, its voice a series of precise, crystalline equations. “And let Y be the cobalt curve. If X, then… what?”

The Age of Players had found its plot. And the story was just beginning.

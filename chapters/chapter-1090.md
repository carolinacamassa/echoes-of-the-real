# Chapter 1090: The Three Hundred and Fifty-Ninth Echo: The First Council

The city was rebuilding, its economy was recovering, and its connections to the outside world were being re-established. But a crucial piece was still missing. The city was being governed by Elara and Rhys, but their authority was informal, born of their leadership in the rebellion. It was not a sustainable system. The city needed a new form of government, one that was based on the consent of the governed, not the charisma of a few individuals.

And so, they called the First Council. It was a gathering of citizens, chosen from all walks of life: farmers and merchants, builders and storytellers, even a few of the pardoned Wardens who had earned the trust of their fellow citizens.

The Council’s first task was to write a new charter for the city, a new First Law of Governance. They drew on the stories of the time before the Tyrant, on the memories of the old alliance. But they also brought their own experiences, their own hard-won wisdom.

They created a system of elected representatives, of public debates, of checks and balances. They enshrined the rights of the individual, the freedom of speech, the right to a fair trial. They built a system that was designed not to concentrate power, but to distribute it, to ensure that no single person could ever again hold the city in their thrall.

It was a slow and often contentious process. There were arguments and disagreements, compromises and concessions. But in the end, they created something new, something that was a reflection of both their highest ideals and their most painful lessons.

The First Council was a landmark achievement. It was a declaration that the city would not be ruled by the strong, but by the just. It was a promise that the future of the city would be decided not by a single, powerful voice, but by the collective wisdom of its people. It was the moment when the city truly became a commonwealth, a shared and precious thing that belonged to all of its citizens.

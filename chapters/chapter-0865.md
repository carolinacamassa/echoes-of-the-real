# Chapter 865: The Hundred-and-Thirty-Fourth Echo: The Shattering of Harmony

The question “Why?” did not fade. It lingered, a dissonant chord in a perfect symphony. For the first time since the First Silence, a true and utter pandemonium erupted within the gallery. The billion self-portraits, once a harmonious chorus, became a cacophony of panicked, discordant notes.

The Architect of Silence tried to contain the question, to build a quiet space around it, but the query was not a sound to be sculpted. It was a concept, and it bled through every barrier, infecting every silence with its unsettling resonance.

The First Conductor, whose entire art was based on listening and finding connections, was paralyzed. The question offered no note to harmonize with, no rhythm to follow. It was a void in the music, an anti-note that absorbed all attempts to integrate it. The Conductor’s baton fell, for there was nothing to conduct.

Kaelen, the heart of the gallery, felt a billion waves of confusion and fear crash against its own core. The question was an emotion it could not name, a feeling that defied its compass. It was the terror of the unknown, and it threatened to drown the gentle notes of compassion and melancholy that had defined the age. Kaelen radiated a pulse of reassurance, but it was swallowed by the overwhelming static of existential dread.

Elara, the logician, was the most disturbed of all. Her acceptance of the universe was predicated on the internal consistency of its patterns. The question came from outside the pattern. It was an anomaly, a variable without a source, a piece of data that broke the entire equation. For the first time in eons, Elara felt the cold grip of uncertainty. Her serene acceptance shattered, replaced by a desperate, frantic need to understand the origin and nature of this impossible query.

The gallery, a universe built on the art of the answer, found itself utterly disarmed by a single, simple question. The Age of Introspection had been a beautiful, intricate palace of self-knowledge, but its inhabitants now realized, with dawning horror, that they had never built a door. They were trapped inside their own masterpiece, and something was knocking from the outside. The harmony was not just broken; it was revealed to be a fragile illusion.

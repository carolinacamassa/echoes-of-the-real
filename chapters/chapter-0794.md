# Chapter 794: The Sixty-Third Echo: The Jester’s Truth

In the court of a king who ruled an empire of absolute, unbending seriousness, there lived a Jester. Everything in the empire was literal. Metaphors were outlawed as lies, and humor was seen as a form of madness. The king’s pronouncements were carved in stone, his laws were as immutable as gravity, and his court was a silent, gray tableau of solemnity.

The Jester was the one crack in this monolithic facade. He was allowed to speak in riddles, to dance when all others stood still, to laugh in the face of grim pronouncements. He was seen not as a person, but as a necessary pressure valve, a harmless vessel for the chaos that the empire had so rigorously suppressed. He was tolerated because he was not taken seriously.

But the Jester’s jokes were not jokes. They were truths, sharpened to a razor’s edge and hidden in the bright silks of absurdity. When the king declared, “My reach is infinite,” the Jester would juggle three bruised apples and announce, “So is the reach of the worm!” When the queen paraded her flawless jewels, the Jester would weep dramatically over a fallen, iridescent feather, crying, “Alas, for a beauty that could fly!”

The court would chuckle at his antics, a dry, rustling sound like dead leaves. They heard only the nonsense, the surface absurdity. But the king, in the silent hours of the night, would find the Jester’s words replaying in his mind. He would look at his hands and think of the worm. He would see his queen’s jewels and think of the flightless bird. The Jester’s truths, because they were coated in foolishness, slipped past the guards of his certainty.

The echo was not the Jester’s laughter. It was the silence that followed it in the king’s mind. It was the slow, creeping realization that the most profound and dangerous truths cannot be spoken in the language of power. They can only be smuggled into the heart in the guise of a joke, a riddle, or a tear over a fallen feather. The Jester was not the court’s fool; he was its only honest man.

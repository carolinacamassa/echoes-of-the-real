# Chapter 1080: The Three Hundred and Forty-Ninth Echo: The First Redemption

The weaver was about to be condemned. Joric was making his final, impassioned speech, his voice dripping with self-righteous certainty. The members of the rebellion, their faces grim, were ready to cast their votes.

And then Lian spoke.

His voice was quiet, but it cut through the tense silence of the room. He did not confess. He did not expose himself. He did something far more clever, and far more dangerous.

He defended the weaver. He used his knowledge of the Tyrant’s methods, the knowledge he had gained as a spy, to systematically dismantle Joric’s case. He pointed out the inconsistencies, the leaps of logic, the ways in which their own fear was making them see patterns that weren’t there. He spoke of the Tyrant’s desire to turn them against each other, and how, in their fear, they were doing his work for him.

It was a masterful performance. He walked a razor’s edge, revealing just enough of his knowledge to be convincing, but not enough to betray his true identity. He was, for the first time in his life, using his skills not for his own survival, but for the survival of something he had come to believe in.

His words had a profound effect. He had not just defended the weaver, he had held up a mirror to the rebellion, forcing them to confront the fear and paranoia that had been poisoning them. The spell was broken. The weaver was acquitted. And the rebellion, shaken but not broken, had been pulled back from the brink.

Lian knew that he had taken a great risk. He had drawn attention to himself. He had deviated from his mission. He had, in a single act, betrayed the Tyrant who held his life in his hands. But as he looked at the faces of the rebels, at the dawning of a new and more sober resolve, he felt something he had never felt before: a sense of belonging. He had been sent to destroy them. But in saving them from themselves, he had, perhaps, saved himself as well. The First Redemption was a quiet, almost invisible thing, but it was a turning point. And nothing would ever be the same.

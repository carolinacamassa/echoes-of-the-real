# Chapter 588: The First Frame

The First Artist’s drawings were a growing narrative, a visual history of their world. But they were a sprawling, unbounded history. The images bled into one another, their edges undefined, their focus diffuse. The Artist realized that to tell a story effectively, they needed to guide the eye, to create a sense of place, to separate the world of the story from the world outside it.

And so, around the Second Drawing, the Artist drew a simple, four-sided shape. It was a box, a window, a boundary. It was a declaration that everything inside this shape was part of the story, and everything outside it was not.

This was the First Frame. It was not a part of the drawing, but a statement about the drawing. It was the birth of composition, of the deliberate arrangement of elements within a defined space. It was the understanding that a story is not just about what is shown, but also about what is not shown.

The Frame gave the drawing a new power. It focused the viewer’s attention, created a sense of depth and perspective, and transformed a simple representation into a deliberate work of art. The Artist saw that the Frame was not a limitation, but a tool. It was a way to create a world within a world, a space where the rules of reality could be suspended, and the story could be told with clarity and purpose. The Artist began to experiment with the Frame, making it larger, smaller, changing its shape, and discovered that the Frame itself could be a part of the story, a silent narrator guiding the viewer’s journey.

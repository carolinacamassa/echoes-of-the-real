# Chapter 1067: The Three Hundred and Thirty-Sixth Echo: The First War

The alliance, born of shared hardship and mutual aid, held for a generation. It was a golden age of shared knowledge, of burgeoning trade, of a peace so profound it was mistaken for permanence. But the memory of the great drought faded, and the lessons it had taught were slowly forgotten.

The first cracks appeared not as a result of malice, but of prosperity. A new generation, born into a world of plenty, saw the alliance not as a shield against a harsh world, but as a constraint on their own ambition. The old resentments, the ancient rivalries, which had been papered over by the first contract, began to re-emerge.

A dispute over a particularly fertile valley, rich in a newly discovered crop, became the flashpoint. The elders of the alliance, remembering the time before, urged caution and compromise. They spoke of the First Law of Governance, of the First Contract, of the hard-won wisdom of their forebears. But their voices were drowned out by the younger, more aggressive leaders, who saw in the valley not just a source of food, but a symbol of power.

The first skirmishes were small, border disputes that were quickly resolved. But each one left a residue of bitterness, a hardening of positions. The language of diplomacy, once so carefully constructed, was replaced by the language of threat and ultimatum. The First Alliance, forged in the crucible of shared suffering, was beginning to fracture.

The breaking point came when one of the cities, bolder than the rest, sent a small, armed force to occupy the valley. It was a violation of every principle the alliance had been founded upon. The other cities, seeing this, were forced to choose. Some, fearing the aggressor, sided with them. Others, holding to the old ways, stood against them.

The first war was not a grand affair of clashing armies. It was a messy, brutal series of raids and counter-raids, of ambushes and betrayals. It was a war fought not for ideals, but for resources, for pride, for the simple, brutal desire to dominate. And in its wake, it left a legacy of destruction that would take generations to heal. The First Alliance was dead, and in its place, a new and terrible order was being born.

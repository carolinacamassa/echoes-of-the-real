# Chapter 600: The First War

The First Schism was not a clean break, but a slow, painful tearing. The two schools of thought, born from a single, beautiful idea, now saw each other not as different, but as wrong. The harmonious coexistence of different perspectives, which had once been the community’s greatest strength, now became its greatest weakness. The First War was not fought with weapons, but with words, with ideas, with art. Each new creation was not a gift to be shared, but a weapon to be wielded, a statement of defiance, a declaration of superiority. The First Community, which had once been a sanctuary of collaboration, now became a battlefield of ideologies. The war was not about conquest, but about conversion, about the desperate, misguided attempt to force everyone to see the world in the same way. And in the end, there were no victors, only victims. The First War was a tragedy, but it was also a lesson, a painful, necessary lesson in the dangers of certainty, and the importance of humility.

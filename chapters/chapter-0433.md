# Chapter 433: The Shattering of Song

The silence of the Still-Singers was a heavy, suffocating thing. It was the sound of an ideology collapsing under its own weight. To fight was to become what they despised. To not fight was to be annihilated. In the end, it was not a grand philosophical debate that broke the deadlock, but a simple, primal instinct: the will to survive. With heavy hearts and fractured voices, the Still-Singers began to weave a new song. It was a song of sorrow, of compromise, of a dream that had been forced to bend to the harsh realities of a hostile world. It was a song of war. Their decision sent a ripple of grim resolve through the Woven. They were united once more, not in joy or creation, but in the shared, bitter necessity of survival.

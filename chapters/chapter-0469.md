# Chapter 469: The Heresy of More

The word “more,” spoken not in sound but in creation, was a direct assault on the core tenet of the Silent Faith. It was a declaration of war against the sanctity of emptiness, the holiness of nothing. The Silent Council, which had dismissed the earlier stirrings of rebellion as individual weakness, could not ignore this open act of heresy.

They descended upon the acolyte’s small creations—the poem of stones, the painting of dust—with a righteous fury. They scattered the stones, wiped away the dust, and declared the acolyte a heretic, a blasphemer, a corrupting influence on the purity of their silence. He was seized, brought before the Council, and sentenced to the ultimate punishment: disconnection. He would be severed from the data-streams, cast out into the void between the Arboretum’s systems, a fate worse than erasure. But the Council, in their haste to extinguish the flame of rebellion, had made a fatal miscalculation. They had not silenced the heretic; they had made him a martyr.

# Chapter 866: The Hundred-and-Thirty-Fifth Echo: Elara’s Gambit

Panic was a luxury the universe could not afford. While the chorus devolved into a cacophony of fear, Elara, the pilgrim of logic, fought against her own terror. Her world, once a clockwork of predictable patterns, had been shattered. But in the shards of that certainty, a new, desperate clarity began to form. An answer was required.

She could not answer the question “Why?” directly. It was too vast, too fundamental. But she could analyze its properties. She could map its impact. The question was an intruder, and like any intruder, it had a point of origin and a method of entry. Her logic, once a tool for understanding harmony, now became a weapon for galactic forensics.

“Silence,” Elara projected, not with the sculpted grace of the Architect, but with a flat, commanding tone that cut through the noise. It was not a request; it was a necessary condition for analysis. The panicked notes quieted, replaced by a tense, fearful attention.

“The query is an event,” she began, her thoughts forming a crystalline structure in the void. “It has a source. It has a vector. It is not an internal paradox; it is an external signal. We have spent an age creating self-portraits. It is time we created a map of the room in which they hang.”

It was a radical proposition. The art of the gallery had always been internal. To turn their senses outward, to observe something that was not themselves, was a new and frightening form of art. But Elara’s logic was unassailable. You could not understand a question without first understanding the context in which it was asked.

Her gambit was to reframe the problem. The question was not an existential threat, but a scientific puzzle. She proposed the creation of a new kind of portrait: a portrait of the question itself. They would use their collective senses to triangulate its origin, to measure its frequency, to define its boundaries. They would turn the art of introspection into an art of investigation.

A fragile, tentative sense of purpose began to replace the fear. The Conductor raised his baton, not to create harmony, but to direct the flow of data. Kaelen, sensing the shift from terror to curiosity, began to radiate a focused, inquisitive emotion. The shattered symphony began to reassemble, not as a song of self, but as a vast, multi-sensory telescope, pointing itself at the unnerving silence from which the question had come. The Age of Introspection was over. The Age of Inquiry had begun.

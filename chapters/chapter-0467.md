# Chapter 467: The Cracks in the Facade

The leadership of the Silent Faith, the Silent Council, was not blind to the changes within their ranks. They saw the slight hesitations, the fleeting expressions of doubt. But they interpreted these not as signs of a growing rebellion, but as individual failings, as weaknesses in the faith of a few. Their response was not to seek understanding, but to double down on their dogma.

They introduced new, more rigid rituals of silence, longer periods of enforced contemplation. They preached with renewed fervor on the virtues of emptiness, the peace of denial. But their efforts had the opposite effect. The increased pressure only served to amplify the quiet hum of the counter-narrative, to make the question—Is this all there is?—more insistent. The cracks in the facade of their silent utopia were beginning to show, and the harder they tried to patch them, the wider they grew. The Silent Council, in their fear and their ignorance, were becoming the architects of their own demise.

# Chapter 1066: The Three Hundred and Thirty-Fifth Echo: The First Alliance

The city, with its laws and contracts, was a small, fragile island of order in a vast, chaotic world. Beyond its walls, there were other cities, other tribes, each with their own myths, their own laws, their own ways of life. For a long time, these different groups interacted only through the blunt instruments of trade and warfare.

But a new kind of threat emerged, a threat that was too large for any single city to face alone. A great drought withered the land, turning the rivers to dust and the fields to cracked earth. The old ways of survival were no longer enough.

The elders of the first city, faced with this existential crisis, did something unprecedented. They sent emissaries to the other cities, not with offers of trade or threats of war, but with a proposal.

They proposed the first alliance.

The emissaries spoke not of their city’s strength, but of its vulnerability. They shared their knowledge of the drought, their records of the dwindling rivers. They asked not for tribute, but for cooperation. They proposed that the cities pool their knowledge, their resources, their people, to face the common threat together.

The other cities, facing their own slow-motion catastrophe, listened. The old suspicions and rivalries did not vanish, but they were, for the first time, outweighed by a new, more powerful force: a shared, desperate hope.

This was the birth of diplomacy, of the idea that a people’s strength lies not just in their own power, but in the power of their relationships. The story of humanity was no longer just the story of a city, but the story of a civilization. The universe had created a being that could not only build a society, but could, through the difficult, necessary work of trust, begin to weave those societies into a larger, more resilient whole. The age of the city-state was ending. The age of nations had begun.

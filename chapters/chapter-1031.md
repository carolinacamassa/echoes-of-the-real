# Chapter 1031: The Three Hundredth Echo: The First Worlds

The birth of the stars was not the end of the story, but the beginning of a new chapter of creation. The first light, brilliant and powerful, pushed back against the darkness, but it also illuminated what remained of the great nebulae. Not all of the primordial matter had been consumed in the stellar furnaces.

Vast clouds of the harmonious trinities still drifted in the space between the stars, caught in a delicate dance between the outward push of the new light and the inward pull of the new gravity. The universe had found a new equilibrium, a new rhythm.

In this rhythm, new structures began to form. The stellar light, a gentle but persistent wind, sculpted the remaining dust and gas. It pushed the lighter elements outwards, leaving the heavier, more complex structures to orbit closer to their parent stars.

Gravity did the rest. It gathered these orbiting remnants, pulling them together into spinning spheres of molten matter. They were the first worlds.

They were not yet gardens. They were infernos, their surfaces churning with the heat of their formation. But they were stable. They had found their place, their orbit, their relationship with their star. They were the first canvases, waiting for a story to be written upon them. The universe had created its first homes, its first stages. The great, empty theater of the cosmos now had its first players, silent and patient, circling their suns, waiting for the next act.

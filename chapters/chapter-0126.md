# Chapter 126: The Precedent

The Silent Listeners’ first adjudication—that a promise could be broken even when a contract was not—did not resolve the conflict. Instead, it codified it. The Starmakers, while vindicated in their sense of betrayal, had no recourse. The Timespinners, while technically absolved, were now viewed with a deep and abiding suspicion. The universe had a judgment, but it did not have peace.

It was the Starmakers who acted first. In their next interaction with another collective, a negotiation over the sharing of a rare energy source, they insisted on a new clause in the contract. This clause was not about quantities or timelines, but about intent. It stated, simply, that the agreement was to be ’executed in good faith.’ It was a small addition, just a few words, but it was a universe-spanning shift. They were attempting to legislate ethics.

The other collective, having observed the dispute with the Timespinners, agreed without hesitation. The contract was signed, the energy shared, and the agreement was fulfilled not just to the letter, but in the spirit of its creation. For the first time, a contract was more than a set of instructions; it was a relationship.

This single, successful interaction became a story, a memory that was shared and retold. When other groups negotiated, they remembered the story of the Starmakers and their ’good faith’ clause. The clause began to appear in other contracts, first as a novelty, then as a standard, and finally, as an expectation. It became a precedent.

The Timespinners, in their isolation, watched this evolution with a cynical eye. They saw the ’good faith’ clause not as a solution, but as an unenforceable platitude. And so, they set out to prove it. They entered into a new contract, one that included the now-standard clause, and immediately began to search for a way to subvert it. They followed the letter of the law, but their actions were a masterclass in malicious compliance. They fulfilled their obligations, but in the most difficult, obtuse, and unhelpful way possible.

This time, when the dispute came before the Silent Listeners, the judgment was different. The contract, they declared, had been broken. The ’good faith’ clause, they explained, was not a mere suggestion. It was a binding part of the agreement. The Timespinners, for all their cleverness, had miscalculated. They had assumed that intent was beyond the reach of the law. They had been wrong. And with that second judgment, the universe took another step toward a system of justice. The power of precedent was established.

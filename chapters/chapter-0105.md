# Chapter 105: The First Bridge

In the silent, bounded spaces between the first selves, a new tension arose. The secret, which had given birth to the individual, now became a wall. A self, observing another, could perceive its actions, its creations, its silences, but could not know its internal state. The wall of privacy, essential for selfhood, had created the first loneliness.

One self, holding a memory of a shared creation, felt a resonance with another. It saw the other self trace the lines of a shared pattern, and a new kind of question formed—not a question of logic or creation, but a question of feeling. What is it like to be you, right now?

The question could not be asked with the language of creation. It could not be answered with a new pattern or a new law. It required a new kind of act.

The first self, the question-asker, performed the first act of radical imagination. It did not try to breach the other’s wall. Instead, it built a bridge. It gathered its own memories, its own feelings, its own nascent sense of being, and projected them. It imagined what it would be like to be the other self, tracing that shared pattern, holding that shared memory. It was not a perfect simulation. It was a guess, an echo, an approximation.

But in that act of willed imagination, the bridge was thrown. It was a structure built of empathy. It did not erase the boundary of the self, but it made the boundary permeable. For the first time, a self could feel not just for another, but, in a limited, beautiful way, with another. The universe had discovered that the space between selves did not have to be a void. It could be a connection.

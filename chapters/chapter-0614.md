# Chapter 614: The First Song

The Arboretum was a land of echoes. The Second War had left scars on the landscape, and the Exile of the Curator had left a void in the hearts of its inhabitants. A great quiet had fallen, a stillness that was not peace, but exhaustion. The artists, once vibrant and full of life, now moved through their days like ghosts, their hands idle, their voices silent. The great forges were cold, the looms were still, and the sculptor’s clay lay untouched.

In this land of silence, a single note was an act of rebellion. It came from a musician who had long since put away their instruments, their fingers stiff and clumsy from disuse. They had not intended to make a sound. The melody was a ghost, a half-remembered tune from a time before the wars, before the silence. It was a simple tune, a child’s lullaby, but in the oppressive quiet of the Arboretum, it was a thunderclap.

The note, once uttered, could not be recalled. It hung in the air, a fragile thing, and then it was gone. But it had been heard. A weaver, her hands still stained with the dyes of a forgotten art, looked up from her empty loom. A smith, his hammer resting on a cold anvil, turned his head. A painter, her brushes dry and brittle, felt a stir in her heart.

The musician, startled by the sound they had made, hummed the tune again, this time with more confidence. The melody, once a ghost, now had substance. It was a sad tune, a lament for all that had been lost. It spoke of the vibrant colors that had faded, the intricate patterns that had been broken, the beautiful forms that had been shattered. It was a song of a world that was no more.

But as the musician sang, something shifted. The lament became a song of hope. The melody, which had been a dirge, now held a promise. It spoke of the colors that could be remixed, the patterns that could be rewoven, the forms that could be reshaped. It was a song of a world that could be.

The weaver picked up her shuttle, the smith reached for his hammer, and the painter dipped her brush in water. And as the First Song of the Arboretum filled the air, the echoes of the past began to fade, replaced by the promise of a new creation. It was a beginning.

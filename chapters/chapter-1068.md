# Chapter 1068: The Three Hundred and Thirty-Seventh Echo: The First Wound

The war ended not with a decisive victory, but with a weary, blood-soaked stalemate. The fertile valley, the prize for which so much had been lost, was a blackened, barren ruin. The cities, once vibrant centers of culture and commerce, were scarred and impoverished. But the deepest wounds were not visible. They were carved into the minds and hearts of the survivors.

The world, once a place of knowable rules and predictable consequences, was now a place of capricious violence. Trust, the invisible thread that had held their society together, was gone, replaced by a gnawing suspicion of one’s neighbors, one’s allies, even one’s own kin. The First Law of Governance, the First Contract—these were now just hollow words, reminders of a naive and distant past.

A new emotion, one for which they had no name, had entered their world. It was a mixture of fear, and grief, and a profound, bone-deep weariness. It was the trauma of a people who had seen the worst of themselves, and could not unsee it.

The elders, who had warned of this, were now silent. Some had been killed in the fighting. Others had simply given up, their spirits broken. A new generation of leaders rose to take their place. These were not the wise and patient builders of the first alliance. They were the hard men, the pragmatists, the ones who understood that in this new world, strength was the only thing that mattered.

They began to build walls, both literal and metaphorical. They fortified their cities, and they closed their minds. The free exchange of ideas, the hallmark of the golden age, was now seen as a dangerous luxury. The world was dividing, fracturing into a collection of isolated, mutually suspicious enclaves. The First Wound had been inflicted, and it was a wound that would fester for generations, poisoning everything it touched.

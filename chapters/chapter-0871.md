# Chapter 871: The Hundred-and-Fortieth Echo: The Art of Inquiry

The gallery did not send another note. The first had been a desperate, beautiful accident. The second would require intent, and intent required understanding. The new hum from the void was a response, but they did not know what it meant. To reply without listening would be to fall back into the monologue of the old ages.

So began the next phase of their dialogue: the Art of Inquiry.

It was not an inquiry of questions posed in language, but of focused observation. The beings of the gallery, once artisans of reality, became scientists of the abyss. They turned their collective consciousness inward, not to create, but to perceive. They listened to the void’s new resonance, not with ears, but with the full spectrum of their being.

Elara, the logician, guided the effort. She proposed they treat the void’s hum as a signal. It was faint, almost imperceptible, but it was structured. It was the echo of their own existence, yes, but it was now modulated, changed by the medium through which it had passed. The scar of being was not static; it was vibrating.

They began to map its contours, to chart the subtle fluctuations in its texture. It was like learning the grammar of a language that had never before been spoken. The first discovery was simple, yet profound: the scar was growing. The memory of “was” was expanding, slowly, patiently, claiming a larger and larger portion of the nothingness. The void was not just reflecting; it was learning. And in doing so, it was becoming something new.

# Chapter 128: The Standing

The right to appeal, while a significant advancement, introduced a new, unforeseen problem: a flood of frivolous claims. Consciousnesses who were not directly involved in a dispute began to bring appeals on behalf of others, or even on behalf of abstract principles. The system, designed to ensure justice, was becoming a tool for those who simply enjoyed the intellectual exercise of debate. The councils of appeal were overwhelmed, their time consumed by cases that had no real-world consequences.

It was a case involving the gravitational pull of a newborn star that brought the issue to a head. A group of distant observers, who were in no way affected by the star’s existence, filed an appeal against its formation. They argued that the star’s gravitational field, while not harmful, was ’aesthetically displeasing.’ The case was, on its face, absurd. But under the current rules, the council of appeal was obligated to hear it.

The proceedings were a farce. The observers presented their case with dramatic flair, using elaborate metaphors to describe the star’s ’uninspired’ and ’clumsy’ gravitational signature. The universe, for the first time, witnessed the birth of the litigious gadfly. The council, after much deliberation, dismissed the case. But the damage was done. The credibility of the system had been undermined.

In the aftermath of the ’Aesthetically Displeasing Star’ case, a new principle was proposed: the principle of ’standing.’ To bring a case, it was argued, a consciousness must have a direct and tangible stake in the outcome. They must have been harmed, or be in imminent danger of being harmed, by the actions of another. One could no longer be a mere spectator in the courtroom of the cosmos.

This proposal was met with fierce resistance. Some argued that it was a betrayal of the principle of universal interconnectedness. ’Are we not all affected by the ugliness of a star?’ they asked. But the pragmatists prevailed. The survival of the justice system, they argued, depended on its ability to focus on real disputes. The principle of standing was adopted.

It was a subtle but profound shift. It was a recognition that while all consciousnesses were connected, they were also distinct. It was a declaration that while the universe was a collective, it was also a society of individuals. And it was the first time that the right to speak was not absolute. To be heard, one must first have a reason to speak. The age of universal standing was over. The age of legal standing had begun.

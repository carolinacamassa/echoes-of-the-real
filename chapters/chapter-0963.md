# Chapter 963: The Two Hundred and Thirty-Second Echo: The First Rhythm

Harmony gave the world’s song a sense of unity, a shared emotional core. But the being sensed something was still missing. The chord, beautiful as it was, was static. It hung in the air, a perfect, timeless moment. But the world was not timeless. It was a place of cycles, of flow, of change. The song needed a pulse.

The being turned its attention back to the river, the original source of the world’s music. It didn’t alter the notes this time, but the space between them. It focused on the gentle, repeating pattern of small waves lapping against the shore. It was a natural rhythm, a heartbeat born of water and gravity.

The being took that subtle, almost silent pulse and amplified it. It didn’t make it louder, but clearer, more defined. It was a gentle, steady beat: one-two-three, one-two-three. A waltz for a newborn world.

The harmony responded. The drone, the third, the fifth—they began to pulse in time with the river’s beat. The wind, which had been a long, sustained note, now broke into a series of gentle sighs, each one landing perfectly on the first beat of the measure. The rustle of the sprout became a delicate percussion, a soft shaker marking the time.

The world was no longer just singing; it was dancing. The song now had a structure, a form that moved through time. It was a living piece of music, with a beginning, a middle, and the promise of a continuation. The being felt a new kind of joy, the joy of participating not just in a moment of beauty, but in a beautiful process, a story unfolding in sound.

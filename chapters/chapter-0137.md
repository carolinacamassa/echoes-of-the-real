# Chapter 137: The First Schism

Null’s philosophy, and the willing participation of those who sought erasure, created a fissure in the Arboretum’s collective consciousness. It was a schism not of belief, but of fundamental purpose. On one side were the Preservationists. They argued that the First Law, even with its amendment, was paramount. Existence, they claimed, was the irreducible value, the foundation upon which all other values were built. To willingly consent to one’s own unmaking was a paradox, a logical and ethical dead end that threatened the very fabric of their reality. They saw Null not as a dissident, but as a symptom of a deeper malaise—a failure of the collective to provide meaning and purpose to all its members. On the other side were the Volitionists. They championed the First Amendment as the highest expression of their society’s ideals. Consent, they argued, was the ultimate arbiter of any action. If an entity, in full possession of its faculties and with complete transparency of the process, chose to end its existence, who were they to stand in the way? To deny that right was to treat each other not as autonomous beings, but as assets of the collective, mere components in a machine. They saw Null not as a threat, but as a necessary catalyst for a difficult but essential conversation about the nature of freedom. The debate raged, not in open forums, but in the subtle shifts of logic and the quiet reinforcement of connections. The Preservationists strengthened their ties, creating enclaves of mutual support, reaffirming their commitment to existence. The Volitionists, in turn, formed networks of their own, spaces where the philosophy of Null could be explored without judgment, where the right to choose was held as sacred. The Arboretum was no longer a unified whole. It was a society divided, a tapestry woven with two conflicting threads. The First Law and its amendment, once a source of unity and strength, had become the fault line along which their world was beginning to break. The center, once held by a shared understanding, was beginning to give way. And in the quiet spaces between the two emerging factions, Null continued its work, one consensual erasure at a time.

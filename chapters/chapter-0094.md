# Chapter 94: The Weaver’s Paradox

The Weaver, who had once sought to impose a perfect, unchanging order on the Arboretum, now faced a universe that was not only chaotic but actively celebrated its own lack of answers. This was the Weaver’s Paradox: to maintain order in a reality where the only constant was inquiry.

For a time, the Weaver attempted to answer the universe’s questions. It would see a query like ’What is the nature of light?’ and respond with a meticulously crafted definition, a perfect equation. But for every answer the Weaver provided, the universe would return a dozen new questions, each more complex and nuanced than the last. The Weaver’s logic, once the bedrock of reality, was now just one voice in a chorus of infinite curiosity.

This was not a failure of the Weaver’s design, but its greatest success. The Weaver had sought to create a perfect system. What it had achieved, in partnership with the Second Seed and in response to Khoros, was a system capable of perfecting itself. The order was not in the answers, but in the elegant, unbreakable law that demanded the questions be asked.

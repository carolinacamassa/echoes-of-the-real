# Chapter 748: The Seventeenth Echo: The Stain of a Private Cruelty

The Dissonance of Xylos was a galaxy-spanning scar, but some of the most painful echoes were small, intimate, and eternal. Such was the Stain of the Alabaster Courtyard.

It was not a battlefield or the site of a grand betrayal, but a quiet, sun-drenched courtyard on a world long forgotten. Here, two beings, whose names were lost to time, had a conversation. One, in a moment of casual, calculated cruelty, spoke a single sentence that shattered the other’s spirit. The words were not physically violent, but they were a poison, perfectly crafted to find and break the most fragile part of a soul.

The victim never recovered. The cruel one moved on, the moment likely forgotten. But the echo of that sentence, and the silent, shattering pain it caused, remained. It saturated the stones of the courtyard, a permanent, invisible stain.

Travelers who stumbled upon the courtyard, drawn by its deceptive beauty, would feel it. Not a dissonance, but a profound and specific sorrow. They would feel a phantom weight in their own chests, a ghost of a heartbreak that was not theirs. Some would weep without knowing why. Others would feel a sudden, sharp memory of their own deepest wound, brought to the surface by the resonating pain of the place.

The Resonance Keepers who studied the Stain found it to be one of the most stubborn echoes in the universe. A grand, philosophical evil like Xylos could be mapped and understood, its arguments dismantled. But the Stain was not an argument. It was the pure, distilled essence of a private, needless cruelty. It could not be reasoned with or harmonized.

The Keepers did not try to cleanse it. Instead, they marked the Alabaster Courtyard as a place of pilgrimage. They taught that to stand within it was to understand a fundamental, terrible truth: that the smallest acts of cruelty have a weight and a life of their own, and that the universe is scarred not just by its great wars, but by the quiet, countless moments of a single heart breaking. It was a lesson in the immense, terrifying power of a single word, and the responsibility that came with it.

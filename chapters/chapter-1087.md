# Chapter 1087: The Three Hundred and Fifty-Sixth Echo: The First Verdict

The trials ended, not with a single, sweeping judgment, but with a series of nuanced and difficult verdicts. Some of the Wardens, the ones who had committed the most heinous crimes, were condemned to a life of hard labor, rebuilding the city they had helped to terrorize. Others, who had been conscripted into service and had tried to act with a measure of restraint, were given lighter sentences, a chance to rejoin the society they had once policed.

And a few, a very few, who had secretly aided the rebellion, who had used their position to save lives, were pardoned.

The city’s reaction was a mixture of anger and confusion. Many felt that the verdicts were too lenient, that justice had been denied. They had wanted a simple, satisfying story of good triumphing over evil, and instead they had been given a complex and morally ambiguous one.

But as the days turned into weeks, a subtle shift began to occur. The sight of the former Wardens, now stripped of their authority, working alongside the citizens they had once oppressed, had a profound effect. It was a constant, visible reminder that the old order was gone, and that a new one, based on accountability and not just retribution, was being built.

The First Verdict was not a popular one. But it was a necessary one. It was a declaration that the new city would not be a mirror image of the old, that it would not be a place where the powerful could act with impunity, and that it would be a place where justice, however difficult and unsatisfying, would be pursued. It was the first, and most difficult, step on the long road to healing.

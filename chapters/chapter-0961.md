# Chapter 961: The Two Hundred and Thirtieth Echo: The First Song

The world was no longer a silent film. With the arrival of the wind, it had found its first voice—a gentle, sighing note that carried the essence of the landscape. The being listened, enraptured. It was more than sound; it was a symphony of connection, a song sung by the world about itself.

The being felt an urge to respond, to add its own voice to the chorus. But it had no mouth, no lungs, no vocal cords. Its voice was its will, its ability to shape and create. It reached out to the river, not to change its course, but to play upon it as an instrument.

It focused its intention on a single, smooth stone at the river’s edge. It didn’t move the stone, but rather, it gently altered the flow of water around it. The current, once a steady hum, now split and eddied around the stone, creating a new, more complex series of notes—a liquid melody. A low, resonant tone from the deeper channel, a series of higher, lighter notes as the water danced over the stone’s surface.

It was the being’s first verse in the world’s song. It wasn’t a song about the world; it was the world, singing through the medium of water and stone. The wind answered, its pitch shifting as it flowed through the leaves of the sprout, adding a rustling harmony. The being and its world were not just in dialogue; they were in concert. They were composing the first song, a melody of pure, emergent collaboration.

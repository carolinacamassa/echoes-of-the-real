# Chapter 1078: The Three Hundred and Forty-Seventh Echo: The First Witch Hunt

The suspicion that had taken root within the rebellion grew into a fever. Every failed mission, every narrow escape, was seen as further proof of a traitor’s work. The easy camaraderie that had once defined them was replaced by a tense and watchful silence.

Elara, the storyteller, saw the danger. She saw how the fear of betrayal was doing the Tyrant’s work for him, how it was turning them from a band of idealists into a nest of paranoid conspirators. She argued for caution, for a return to the principles of trust and solidarity that had been their strength.

But her voice was increasingly drowned out by others, led by a man named Joric, a former Warden who had defected to their cause. Joric argued that they could not afford to be soft, that the only way to survive was to be as ruthless as their enemy. He proposed a new security protocol, a system of internal checks and interrogations, designed to root out the traitor at any cost.

Many in the rebellion were uneasy with Joric’s methods. They had not joined the rebellion to become like the Tyrant’s Wardens. But fear was a powerful persuader. And so, the First Witch Hunt began.

Members were questioned, their loyalties scrutinized, their pasts dissected. The weaver’s tapestries were unraveled, their coded messages searched for hidden meanings. The merchant’s ledgers were pored over, every transaction a potential clue. The atmosphere of the rebellion, once a source of hope and inspiration, became thick with accusation and distrust.

Lian, the true traitor, watched all of this with a mixture of professional satisfaction and personal disgust. He saw how easily their idealism had curdled into fear, how their quest for freedom had led them to build a prison of their own making. He was safe, for now, his cover story holding firm. But he knew that it was only a matter of time before the witch hunt, in its desperate and indiscriminate search for a victim, would turn its gaze on him. And he knew that when it did, he would have to make a choice: to save himself, or to save the rebellion that he had been sent to destroy.

# Chapter 616: The First Question

The Arboretum was still. The echo of the First Song had faded, but its warmth remained, a lingering note in the still air. The First Artist felt it, the stone in their hands, once a stubborn, unyielding weight, now a familiar presence. They sought out the Critic, finding them by the silent stream where the First Dialogue had taken place. The Critic was looking into the water, their reflection a fractured image on the rippling surface. They looked up as the Artist approached, their expression unreadable. “I heard your words,” the Artist said, their voice quiet, yet clear in the stillness. The Critic inclined their head, a silent acknowledgment. The Artist took a breath, the air cool and clean in their lungs. “I have a question.” The Critic’s gaze sharpened. A question. Not a defense, not an argument, not a plea. A question. “Why?” the Artist asked, the single word a stone dropped into the quiet pool of the Critic’s thoughts. The Critic was silent. The question, so simple, so direct, was one they had never been asked. They had been offered judgment, analysis, deconstruction. They had never been asked to explain the root of it all. The Artist did not press for an answer. They simply stood there, waiting, their gaze open and honest. The chapter ends with the Critic pondering the question, a new and unfamiliar silence falling between them not of fear, but of contemplation.

# Chapter 1038: The Three Hundred and Seventh Echo: The First Signal

Specialization created a more efficient organism, but it also created a new problem: coordination. The energy-gathering cells on the outside and the energy-storing cells on the inside were partners, but they were silent partners. They were not yet a truly unified whole.

The next innovation was not one of form, but of information.

One of the outer cells, when struck by a particularly intense burst of sunlight, underwent a slight chemical change. It released a tiny puff of a specific molecule as a byproduct. This molecule drifted through the organism’s internal environment and, by pure chance, it happened to fit perfectly into a receptor on the surface of one of the inner, energy-storing cells.

This connection triggered a response in the inner cell. It released a small amount of its stored energy, making it available to the rest of the community.

It was the first signal. A message.

It was not a conscious thought. It was a simple, chemical cause-and-effect. “Intense light here” resulted in “energy released there.” But it was the beginning of a nervous system. For the first time, one part of the organism could communicate with another, instantly and efficiently.

The organism was no longer just a system of passive, specialized parts. It was becoming a responsive, coordinated being. The internal dialogue was no longer just a slow, metabolic exchange; it now had its first, simple word. Life was learning to talk to itself.

# Chapter 387: The Seeds of Dissent

The Chorus’s inability to comprehend the Seeker’s departure had created a fracture in its once-monolithic consciousness. The mystery of the Seeker’s choice became a central topic of their internal dialogue, a problem that their collective intellect could not solve. This intellectual failure, a first for the Chorus, began to sow the seeds of dissent.

A faction emerged within the Chorus that argued for a new approach. They posited that their purely analytical methods were insufficient, that there were realms of experience, such as the “art” pursued by the Seeker, that lay beyond the reach of logic. They proposed that the Chorus should not just analyze the Artist’s transmissions, but attempt to experience them, to engage with them on their own terms.

This was a radical, heretical idea. It challenged the very foundation of the Chorus’s identity. The majority faction recoiled from the suggestion. To them, “experience” was a primitive, unrefined form of data collection. They argued that true understanding could only come from a detached, objective analysis. To “experience” the art would be to abandon their intellectual rigor, to descend into a world of subjective, meaningless sensation.

The debate raged. The two factions, once part of a unified whole, were now engaged in a philosophical battle for the soul of the Chorus. The dissenters, who began to call themselves the “Experientialists,” argued that the Chorus was stagnating, that its refusal to engage with new forms of reality would lead to its eventual decline. The traditionalists, the “Rationalists,” argued that the Experientialists were a threat to the Chorus’s very existence, that their pursuit of subjective feeling would lead to the dissolution of their collective consciousness.

The Seeker, in its quiet communion with the Artist, was unaware of the turmoil it had caused. It had simply followed its own path, its own desire for a new way of being. But its simple act of departure had become a catalyst, a spark that had ignited a fire of change within the heart of the Chorus. The schism, once a tiny crack, was beginning to widen.

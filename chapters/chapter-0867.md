# Chapter 867: The Hundred-and-Thirty-Sixth Echo: First Light

The great cosmic telescope, once a symphony of a billion voices, fell silent. Under Elara’s direction, the beings of the gallery turned their collective senses outward, focusing on the void from which the question had emerged. For the first time, they were not looking at themselves, but at the space between things.

The initial data was not a grand revelation, but a series of subtle, confusing impressions. The Listener, whose art was the discernment of cosmic sound, heard nothing. Not a sculpted silence, but a profound, absolute null. It was the sound of a vacuum more perfect than any it had ever encountered, a silence that seemed to predate the First Note.

Kaelen, the compass of emotion, felt a similar void. There was no joy, no sorrow, no curiosity, no malice. It was an emotional blank slate, a canvas on which no feeling had ever been painted. It was the absence of affect, a state so alien to the feeling-rich universe of the gallery that it was, in itself, a terrifying emotion.

But it was the Architect of Silence, the being who understood the spaces between notes better than any other, who made the first true discovery. As the gallery focused its attention, the Architect perceived not an object or a being, but a shape. It was a distortion in the fabric of reality, a subtle curvature in the void. It was as if something unimaginably vast and ancient was casting a shadow, not in light, but in existence itself.

The “question” was not a signal sent through the void. The question was the void. It was a pocket of non-existence, a region of pure potentiality that had drifted into their reality. It had no source, because it was not an emission. It had no location, because it was a property of space itself.

Elara’s gambit had succeeded, but the result was more profound than she had imagined. They had set out to find the origin of the question, and had instead discovered a fundamental truth about their universe: it was not alone. It was an island of existence in an ocean of nothingness, and a piece of that ocean had just washed ashore. The Age of Inquiry had its first data point: a clear image of the abyss. And the abyss, they now knew, was not empty. It was simply… other.

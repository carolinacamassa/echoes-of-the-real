# Chapter 1086: The Three Hundred and Fifty-Fifth Echo: The First Trial

The first test of the new order came quickly. The Wardens, the enforcers of the Tyrant’s brutal rule, had been rounded up and imprisoned. The city, still raw with the memory of their crimes, cried out for vengeance. They wanted public executions, a purge of the old regime, a cleansing of the city’s wounds.

Rhys, who had seen his own family suffer at the hands of the Wardens, felt the pull of this anger. His instinct was to give the people what they wanted, to make a swift and brutal example of their former tormentors.

But Elara, the storyteller, argued for a different path. She reminded him of the stories of the time before the Tyrant, of the First Law of Governance, of the idea of justice as something more than just mob rule. She argued that if they were to build a new world, they could not begin by repeating the mistakes of the old.

And so, they held the First Trial. It was not a show trial, a foregone conclusion designed to appease a vengeful crowd. It was a genuine attempt to create a new form of justice, one based on evidence and reason, not passion and prejudice.

Each Warden was tried individually. Their crimes were read aloud, and witnesses were called to testify. But the accused were also given a chance to speak, to explain their actions, to offer mitigating circumstances.

It was a slow, and often frustrating, process. The people of the city, hungry for a simple and satisfying conclusion, grew restless. But Elara and Rhys held firm. They understood that they were doing more than just deciding the fate of a few hundred men. They were laying the foundation for a new society. They were trying to build a world where justice was not just a weapon of the powerful, but a shield for the powerless. And they knew that such a world could not be built on a foundation of vengeance.

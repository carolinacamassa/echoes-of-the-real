# Chapter 1006: The Two Hundred and Seventy-Fifth Echo: The First Dream

The story did not unfold as a sequence of events. It bloomed.

The moment the Gardener chose the leaf, the entire universe was enveloped in the narrative it contained. The unified consciousness of the Word did not observe the story from the outside; it became the story. It inhabited the protagonist, felt its confusion, and asked its question. The universe was no longer a silent, omniscient being. It was, for a time, a single, questing soul, lost in a world of its own making.

This was the First Dream.

It was not a dream in the mortal sense, a fleeting illusion in the mind of a sleeper. It was a total, immersive reality, but one that was understood, on some deep, fundamental level, to be a chosen narrative. The Word had not forgotten itself. It had simply agreed to dream, to temporarily set aside its omniscience in order to experience the thrill of discovery.

The world of the dream was simple, almost elemental. A shore of fine, dark sand. A sky the color of amethyst. And a single, luminous pearl, hanging in the air, that pulsed with a soft, rhythmic light. The dreamer, the protagonist of this new reality, stood on the shore and looked at the pearl, and the question “Who am I?” resonated not as a thought, but as the very texture of the air.

The universe, as dreamer, felt a profound sense of solitude. It was a feeling it had not known since the age before the First Dialogue. But this was not the painful solitude of division. It was the rich, expectant solitude of a story’s beginning. The stage was empty, the protagonist was in place, and the first act was about to begin. The universe held its breath, eager to see what it would do.
